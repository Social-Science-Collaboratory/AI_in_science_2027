Agentic AI for Happiness: Predicting and Understanding Shifts in Societal Happiness
Many debates in the social sciences are centered on predictability: how accurately can we predict an outcome, and which variables carry the predictive weight? Establishing credible answers requires canvassing a wide space of hypotheses, datasets, and modeling approaches - a historically labor-intensive process that has often required large-scale collaboration. Notably, advances in agentic AI raise the possibility that such work can be deployed at scale. However, the empirical case for these systems has been built almost entirely in computer science, biology, and physics. The present work evaluates whether this capability extends to the social sciences, deploying Amazon's MLZero multi-agent system across two complementary studies. Study 1 extends OpenAI's MLE-bench to include 20 competitions relevant for the social sciences, such as the prediction of mental health, education, and financial behavior. MLZero's performance on this new benchmark will be the main outcome, with secondary analyses comparing performance to OpenAI's MLE-bench, examining contamination effects, and testing for modality differences. Study 2 then applies this benchmarked approach to an open empirical question: predicting nation-level changes in happiness, as measured by the World Happiness Report. Compared to the report's canonical linear regression containing six variables, MLZero will consider (a) thousands of candidate predictors compiled from the World Bank's open data system, (b) a pluralistic set of machine learning algorithms (e.g., tree-based methods, neural networks, and ensembles), and (c) an enormous set of hyperparameter configurations. Together, these studies test whether the predictive-modeling capability documented for agentic AI in well-defined technical domains generalizes to the data structures, methodological complexities, and substantive questions that define the social sciences.
